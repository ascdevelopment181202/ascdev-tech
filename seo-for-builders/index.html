What does SEO mean for a building firm?

SEO stands for search engine optimisation. For a builder it means your firm appears when a homeowner searches for the kind of project you build, in the place you build it.

A building job is a large decision, and a customer can search more than once on the way to it. They search first to find out what is possible, then to find firms, then to check the firm they have been told about. A builder who only shows up for "builders near me" is present for one of those searches and missing for the rest.

So SEO for a builder is spread across the whole decision. It covers the early questions, the project searches and the search for your own name. The aim is fewer, better enquiries: people who want the work you do and have already seen that you can do it.

Which planning questions should a builder's website answer?

The early questions are about rules, and the official answers are public. A builder's site can point to them and add what the builder knows from doing the work.

  • Planning permission. GOV.UK says you will probably need it to build something new, to make a major change such as an extension, or to change the use of a building. See its page on planning permission in England and Wales.
  • Building regulations. GOV.UK says the regulations cover the construction and extension of buildings, and that you must check whether you need approval before you build. See building regulations approval.

A good answer page states the rule in a line, links to the official source. Then it says how your firm handles it: who draws the plans, who applies, and what the customer has to do. Do not guess at the rules or promise an outcome. That GOV.UK planning page covers England and Wales only, so say which nations you work in.

How do builders show up on Google Maps?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. It also says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help. It says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. All of that is in Google's guide to local ranking.

For a builder that turns into a short list:

  • List each type of work as a service on your Google Business Profile: extensions, loft conversions, renovations, new builds.
  • Add photos of finished projects, and keep adding them as jobs complete.
  • Ask for a review at handover. Google lets you share a link or QR code for this.
  • Keep your name, address and phone number the same on your site, your profile and every directory.

Distance still counts, so expect to show best near your base. Our Google Maps guide goes through each step.

How do finished projects help a builder rank?

A written-up project is one of the most useful pages a builder can add for search. It names the type of work and the town in a natural way, which is what a project search is made of. It gives a search engine or an AI assistant something specific to match: a rear extension in one town, a dormer loft in another.

One project page can then support several others. It links up to the page for that type of work and to the page for that area, and those pages link back to it. Over time each service page is backed by real jobs instead of a paragraph of claims.

The write-up does not need to be long. The job, the place, what the customer wanted, what was tricky and how long it took are enough. We write these with you from your photos and a few notes after each handover.

Do FMB and TrustMark listings help a builder get found?

They give a customer somewhere independent to check you, and they are pages about your firm on sites other than your own.

  • Federation of Master Builders. The FMB runs a public Find a Builder search and says members have to pass a vetting and inspection process before joining.
  • TrustMark. TrustMark describes itself as the Government Endorsed Quality Scheme for work in or around the home, and has a search for registered tradespeople.

Google says prominence is based partly on how many websites link to your business. Nobody outside Google can say how much one listing counts, so treat a membership as proof for customers first.

If you hold one, make sure the listing shows the same name, phone number and website as your Google profile, and say so on your own site. If you do not, we do not invent it. How AI assistants find facts about a firm is covered on our AI search visibility page.

Questions builders ask about SEO

How long does SEO take for a builder?

Plan for three to six months for results to build. Fixes to your Google Business Profile and website can show sooner. Building work has a long lead time as well, so allow for the gap between a first enquiry and a signed job when you judge whether it is working.

Is SEO worth it for a small building firm?

It can be. A small firm that takes on a handful of projects a year does not need a flood of enquiries. It needs a few of the right ones, for the kind of job it wants, in the area it covers. SEO is aimed at exactly that, and the enquiries come to you directly.

Should a builder target "builders near me" or the type of project?

Both, in that order of effort: project type first. A page about loft conversions in your town can answer a loft conversion search better than a general page can. The broad "builders near me" searches are worked on through your Google Business Profile and reviews.

Do I need a page for every town I build in?

Only for the towns you really want work in, and only if each page says something real: projects you have finished there, the kind of houses, how far you travel. A list of town names with the same text on every page is not worth having.

I only finish a few jobs a year. How do I get enough reviews?

Ask every customer, at handover, while the job is fresh. Google lets you send customers a link or a QR code to leave a review. A few detailed reviews that describe the project sit well next to the write-up of that job on your site. Never offer anything in return for a review. Google prohibits it.
