What is SEO for roofers?

SEO stands for search engine optimisation. For a roofer it means being found at the two moments people look for one: when water is coming through the ceiling, and when they have decided the roof needs replacing.

Roofing differs from most trades in one way that shapes the whole plan. A roof is not something people need often, so there are fewer searches than for everyday repairs, but each job is worth far more. One re-roof can be worth more than weeks of small repairs. So roofing SEO is not about the most visitors. It is about being the trusted name in front of a small number of serious buyers.

How do roofers get into the Google Maps results?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that complete and accurate business profiles are more likely to show. It sets this out in its guide to local ranking. For a roofer, that turns into a short list:

  • Set your profile up as a service-area business if you work from home, listing the towns you cover instead of showing your address.
  • List every roofing service by name: repairs, re-roofing, flat roofs, chimneys, guttering.
  • Add photos of real jobs, often. Roofing is visual, and a profile full of recent work stands out from one with a logo and nothing else.
  • Ask every customer for a review, and reply to each one.
  • Keep your name, area and phone number identical on every site that lists you, including your trade body.

How does bad weather change roofing SEO?

Roofing demand comes in waves. After a storm, emergency repair calls come all at once. Through the drier months, people plan and book bigger jobs. A good plan is ready for both.

  • Before the wet season, the emergency and repair pages are in place and the Google profile is up to date, because you cannot rank a new page overnight.
  • During a storm week, a short post on your Google profile says you are taking emergency calls, and missed-call text-back replies to everyone you cannot answer from a roof.
  • In quieter months, we build the re-roofing and flat roofing pages, add project write-ups and gather reviews from the season's jobs.

The firms that do well after a storm are the ones that did the work months before it.

Can AI assistants recommend a roofer?

Yes. People ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity who to trust with a roof, and the assistants name firms. Trust matters even more here than in a normal search, because the question is often worded that way: "a reliable roofer near me".

Assistants look for outside confirmation. That means a clear website, a full Google profile, reviews that describe real jobs, and listings on trusted sites such as a trade body or TrustMark, if you are a member. We make sure they all tell the same story. More on our AI search visibility page.

Questions roofers ask us

How long does SEO take for a roofer?

Plan for three to six months for results to build. Improvements to your Google Business Profile often show first. Because roofing demand is seasonal, the best time to start is a few months before the wet season, so the pages are ranking when the calls come.

Is SEO worth it when one roofing job pays so much?

That is the reason it tends to work well for roofers. You do not need many enquiries, only a few good ones each month, so every extra one counts.

I work from home. Can I still show up on Google Maps?

Yes. Google lets you set your profile up as a service-area business, which shows the towns you cover instead of your home address. Many roofers rank well this way.

Lead sites show above me on Google. Can I compete?

Yes, in two places. The Google Maps results are for local businesses with a profile, which is where a roofer can appear. And specific searches, such as a flat roof system in your town, are ones a good page on your own site can win.

Should I ask customers to add photos to their reviews?

Yes, if they are happy to. A review with a photo of the finished roof is more convincing to the next customer than words alone, and it adds real images of your work to your Google profile.
