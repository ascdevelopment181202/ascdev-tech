AI search visibility for trades

What is AI search visibility?

People used to type a few words into Google and pick from a list. More and more, they ask a full question instead: "Who is a good electrician near me who can fit a car charger?" An AI assistant reads around the web and answers with a short list of named businesses.

AI search visibility is the work of being on that short list. The answer shows a handful of names, not ten pages of results, so being named matters more than it did in a normal search.

It does not replace SEO. It sits on top of it. The same foundations do most of the work, with a few extra jobs that assistants care about more than a classic search engine does.

How do AI assistants decide which tradespeople to name?

An assistant cannot visit your van or see your work. It can only read. So it builds its answer from what is written about you online, by you and by others.

What do Google and OpenAI say about appearing in AI answers?

It is worth knowing what the companies themselves say, because there is a lot of guesswork sold as fact.

  • Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in its AI answers. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show in normal search, and ordinary good SEO practice applies. It also says you do not need special files or markup. This is in Google's guide to AI features and your website.
  • OpenAI says ChatGPT search may pass search queries to third-party search providers such as Bing, and that answers which use search show their sources. This is in its help article on ChatGPT search.

Two practical points follow. First, a site that is not properly indexed cannot be named, so the basics come first. Second, Bing matters. It is easy to set up Google and forget Bing, which is one of the places ChatGPT can look.

Can anyone guarantee a place in ChatGPT's answers?

No, and you should be careful of anyone who says they can. AI answers change from one day to the next, and from one wording of a question to another. Nobody outside the companies that build the assistants controls what they say.

What can be done is to raise the odds, steadily, by making your business the easiest one in your area to find, understand and trust. That is the same thing that wins on Google Maps, which is why we treat the two as one job. We report it as a trend over months, not as a promise for any one day.

Questions about AI search

What is GEO?

GEO stands for generative engine optimisation. It means making a business easy for AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity to find, understand and recommend. It builds on normal SEO and adds extra attention to clear answers, consistent listings and mentions on other sites.

Is AI search replacing Google?

Not for local trades, at least not yet. Google and Google Maps are still where a great many people look for a tradesperson. AI assistants are an extra place customers now look, and Google itself now shows AI answers in its results. The sensible plan covers both.

Do I need a special file on my website for AI?

No. Google says you do not need new machine readable files or special markup to appear in its AI answers. What you need is a site that search engines can crawl and index, with clear content and accurate structured data.

How do you measure AI search visibility?

We ask the main assistants the same set of questions about your trade and area each month, and record whether your business is named. Because answers vary from day to day, we read it as a trend over several months.

Does being on Bing really matter?

Yes. OpenAI says ChatGPT search may use third-party search providers such as Bing. A free Bing Places listing, and a site that Bing has indexed, give ChatGPT another way to find you. It is often overlooked.
