What does a plumber's website need to do?

A plumbing website serves two very different customers, and it has to work for both.

  • The emergency customer. A burst pipe, a leak through the ceiling or no hot water. They are on a phone, they are stressed, and they want three things at once: your number, whether you cover their area, and whether you can come now. They decide in seconds.
  • The planned customer. A new boiler, a bathroom or a heating upgrade. They compare two or three firms over several days. They want photos, reviews, the brands you fit and a clear idea of how quoting works.

A site built like a brochure serves neither. The emergency customer cannot find the number, and the planned customer cannot find the proof. We build the page layout around both from the start.

What proof do customers look for on a plumber's website?

Letting someone into your home to work on gas and water is a matter of trust. Customers look for proof before they ring, and a site that shows it clearly wins the call.

  • Your Gas Safe registration, with the number shown, if you do gas work. Customers can check it on the Gas Safe Register.
  • Manufacturer accreditations, if you hold them, for the boiler brands you install.
  • Trade memberships you belong to, such as WaterSafe or the CIPHE.
  • Google reviews that name the job, such as a boiler swap or a leak fixed the same day.
  • Photos of your own work. Neat pipework under a new boiler sells better than any stock picture.
  • The guarantees and insurance you actually have.

Only show what you hold. A badge you cannot back up does more harm than no badge.

Which searches should a plumber's website be built around?

People search for the problem or the job, plus where they live. So the site is planned around your services and your areas, not around the word "plumber" alone.

  • "Emergency plumber" plus your town
  • "Boiler repair" and "boiler service" plus your town
  • "New boiler" and "boiler installation" searches
  • "Bathroom fitter near me"
  • Problem searches, such as "no hot water" or "radiator not heating up"

The website gives Google something to rank. Getting it ranked is ongoing work, which we cover on our SEO for plumbers page.

How does a plumbing website catch the calls you cannot answer?

Plumbers miss calls for a good reason: you are under a sink or halfway through a boiler swap. An emergency customer who gets no answer rings the next firm on the list.

So we set up two safety nets with the site. Missed-call text-back sends the caller a text straight away, so they know you will ring back. A short quote form catches the people who look in the evening. Both mean an enquiry is not lost just because your hands were full.

Should I put prices on my plumbing website?

Yes, where you can. A call-out charge, an hourly rate or a "from" price for a boiler service helps a customer decide to ring, and it filters out enquiries that were never a fit. If every job is quoted, say how quoting works and how quickly you reply.

Do I need a separate page for each town I cover?

For the main towns you want work in, yes. Each page should say something real about working there, such as the kinds of homes and jobs you see. Copying one page and swapping the town name does not help and can hold the site back.

I do plumbing but not gas work. Does the site still work for me?

Yes. The site should say plainly what you do and do not take on, then focus on your own strengths, such as bathrooms, leaks, drainage or unvented cylinders. Being clear brings better enquiries and saves you calls for work you cannot do.

I already get work from Checkatrade and word of mouth. Why add a website?

Keep both. A website gives people somewhere to check you out after a recommendation, and the enquiries it brings are yours alone. It is also what Google and AI assistants read when they decide which plumbers to show.
