Who is an electrician's website for?

Most electricians have three kinds of customer, and each looks for something different. A good site gives each one a clear way in.

CustomerWhat they want from the site
HomeownersA clear list of jobs you do, proof you are registered, reviews and a quick way to get a quote.
Landlords and letting agentsSafety reports and remedial work, fast turnaround, and a simple way to book several properties.
BusinessesCommercial experience, testing and maintenance, insurance details and a named contact.

Landlords and agents are worth a page of their own. Safety reports come round again and again, so one good agent relationship can bring steady repeat work.

What pages should an electrician's website have?

One page for each job you want more of. A single "services" page with twenty bullet points gives Google and AI assistants nothing to match to a search.

  • Consumer unit upgrades: what a modern board gives a home and what the job involves.
  • Rewires: full and partial, how long they take and how you keep the house liveable.
  • EICR and landlord safety reports: what the report covers and how you handle any remedial work.
  • EV charger installation: the chargers you fit and what you need to know about the property.
  • Fault finding and repairs: tripping circuits, dead sockets and urgent call-outs.
  • Lighting and smart home work, inside and outside.
  • Commercial electrical work, if you take it on.
  • Area pages for the main towns you cover.

If you are moving into solar, batteries or heat pump electrics, those get their own pages too. They are searched for by name.

What proof should an electrician show online?

Customers cannot judge wiring by looking at it. They judge the person. So an electrician's site has to make registration and certification easy to see.

  • Your scheme registration, such as NICEIC or NAPIT, with the logo linked to your listing where the scheme allows it.
  • A plain line on certificates: that the customer receives the right certificate when the work is done.
  • TrustMark or other memberships you hold.
  • Approved installer status for the charger or battery brands you fit.
  • Reviews that name the job, such as a board change or a rewire.
  • Photos of finished boards and tidy installs. A neat consumer unit says more than a paragraph of text.

Show only what you hold, and keep it current. An out-of-date logo is easy for a careful customer to spot.

Which searches should an electrician's website be built around?

"Electrician near me" matters, but the specific searches often bring the better jobs, and they are easier to win.

  • "Consumer unit replacement" or "fuse board upgrade" plus your town
  • "EICR" or "landlord electrical certificate" plus your town
  • "EV charger installer" plus your town
  • "House rewire" searches
  • "Emergency electrician" and problem searches, such as "electrics keep tripping"

The site gives Google a page for each of these. Getting those pages ranked is the ongoing part, which we cover on our SEO for electricians page.

How should an electrician's website handle quotes?

Many electrical jobs can be priced roughly from a few facts and a photo. A good quote form asks for them up front, which saves you a wasted visit.

  • The type of job, picked from a short list.
  • The postcode, so you know it is in your area.
  • A photo of the consumer unit, or of the spot where a charger would go.
  • Whether it is their own home, a rental or a business.

We also set up missed-call text-back, so a call that comes in while you are working at a board still gets a reply.

Should domestic and commercial work be on the same website?

Usually yes, with a clear section for each. Homeowners and businesses look for different things, so each gets its own pages and its own proof. If the two sides trade under different names, separate sites can make sense, and we will tell you which we think fits.

Do I need a page just for landlords?

If you want that work, yes. Landlords and letting agents search for safety reports by name, and they need to know you can handle several properties and any remedial work. A dedicated page lets you answer that directly.

I am newly qualified and have few reviews. Will a website still help?

Yes. A clear site showing your registration, your services and your areas gives a new firm a professional base. We also set up a simple review request for every finished job, so the reviews build steadily from the start.

Can the website help me get EV charger work?

It can. EV charger installation is searched for by name, so it gets its own page covering the chargers you fit and what you need to know about the property. That gives Google and AI assistants a clear reason to show you for those searches.
