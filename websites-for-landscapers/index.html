Websites for landscapers

ASC Development builds websites for UK landscapers, from garden makeovers to patios, driveways and fencing. Landscaping is bought with the eyes, so a landscaper's website is built around large photos of finished work, sorted by the type of job. Ours go live in about ten days, on one monthly price with no contract, and are set up to be found on Google, Google Maps and AI assistants like ChatGPT.

By ASC Development · Updated 12 November 2026

Why is a landscaper's website different?

Nobody needs a new patio in an emergency. A customer decides to spend money on their garden because they have seen something they want. They look at pictures, they imagine their own garden looking like that, and then they get in touch.

So a landscaping website sells with photographs first and words second. The job of the words is to tell Google what each picture shows, and to tell the customer what it would be like to work with you.

It also has to cope with a wide range of work. A firm that lays driveways, builds decking and looks after gardens is really three businesses to a customer, and each one is searched for by a different name.

How should a landscaper show their work online?

One big gallery of mixed photos is an easy mistake to make. A customer who wants a porcelain patio has to scroll past fencing and turf to find one. Sorting the work by type fixes that, and gives Google a clear page for each.

What pages should a landscaping website have?

One page for each kind of work you want, each with its own photos.

  • Patios and paving: the stone and porcelain you lay and how you prepare the base.
  • Driveways: block paving, resin, gravel or tarmac, and how drainage is handled.
  • Garden design and full makeovers, if you offer them.
  • Fencing and gates.
  • Decking and garden structures.
  • Turfing and artificial grass.
  • Garden maintenance, if you take on regular work.
  • Area pages for the main towns you cover.

Be honest about the split between building and maintenance. If you only want big projects, do not give lawn mowing the same space. The site should push the work you want.

What proof matters for a landscaper?

  • Photos of your own jobs. For this trade they are most of the proof.
  • Reviews that mention the job, ideally a season or two after it was finished, when the customer can say it has lasted.
  • Trade memberships you hold, such as the Association of Professional Landscapers or BALI.
  • Approved installer status with the paving or decking brands you fit, if you have it.
  • Your guarantee on the work, stated in plain words.
  • Insurance, and a waste carrier licence if you take waste away.

Show only what you hold. A careful customer will check.

How does a landscaping website deal with the seasons?

Landscaping enquiries follow the weather. People start thinking about the garden when the days get longer, and many want the work done before summer. If the website only gets attention in spring, it is already late.

  • In autumn and winter, add the year's finished projects and build the service pages, so they are ranking before the rush.
  • In early spring, make sure the phone number, the areas and the lead times shown are right.
  • In the busy months, a short quote form and missed-call text-back catch enquiries while you are on site with a digger running.

A good quote form for landscaping asks for the postcode, the type of job, a rough size and a few photos of the garden as it is now. That is often enough to tell whether a visit is worthwhile.

Questions landscapers ask us

How much does a website for a landscaper cost?

ASC Development charges one flat monthly price that covers the website, hosting, SEO, Google Business Profile management and AI visibility work. There is no large upfront build fee and no long contract. We give you the figure on a call, once we know what you need.

My photos are all taken on a phone. Is that good enough?

Yes. A sharp phone photo of your own work, taken in good light, is better than any stock image. Take a "before" shot when you quote, a few during the job and an "after" from the same spot. We handle sizing them for the site.

Should I use Instagram or Facebook instead of a website?

Use them as well, not instead. Social pages are good for showing new work to people who already follow you. A website is what Google and AI assistants read when a stranger searches for a landscaper in your town, and it is the only one of the three you control.

I do both landscaping and garden maintenance. How should the site handle that?

Give each its own section, and lead with the one you most want. Customers looking for a new patio and customers looking for regular gardening search in different ways, so separate pages let you show up for both without mixing the message.

When is the best time to launch a landscaping website?

Autumn or winter, if you can. Search results take three to six months to build, so a site launched in the quiet months is working by spring. A site launched in spring still helps, but mostly for the following year.
