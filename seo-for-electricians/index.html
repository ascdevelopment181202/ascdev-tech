What is SEO for electricians?

SEO stands for search engine optimisation. For an electrician it means being the firm that shows up when a homeowner searches "electrician near me", a landlord searches for a safety report, or someone asks an AI assistant who can fit a car charger.

Electrical work is searched for in a more specific way than most trades. People often know the name of the job they need: a new consumer unit, an EICR, a rewire, an EV charger. That is good news. Specific searches usually have less competition than "electrician" on its own, and the person searching is closer to booking.

Good SEO for an electrician is built on those named jobs, in the towns you want to work in.

How do electricians get into the Google Maps results?

Google says its local results are based mainly on three things: relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. Prominence is how well known your business is, which Google links partly to reviews and to other websites that mention you. Google sets this out in its own guide to local ranking.

You cannot change where your customers are. You can work on the other two:

  • Fill in your Google Business Profile fully. Google says complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show. List every service by name, including EICRs and EV chargers.
  • Pick the right categories, with "Electrician" as the main one.
  • Earn reviews steadily, and reply to them.
  • Match your website to your profile, with a page for each service and area.
  • Keep your details the same everywhere you are listed, including your scheme's own directory.

Should an electrician target domestic, landlord or commercial searches?

All three can work, but not all at once from one page. Each group searches differently and wants different proof.

  • Domestic: the largest number of searches. Won with service pages, reviews and a strong Maps presence.
  • Landlord: fewer searches, but each customer can bring many jobs. Won with a dedicated page and fast, simple booking.
  • Commercial: the fewest searches and the longest sales process. Won with case studies, accreditations and a named contact.

We agree with you which comes first, build that out properly, then add the next. Spreading thin across all three is a common reason an electrician's site ranks for none.

Can AI assistants recommend an electrician?

Yes. People now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity questions like "who can fit an EV charger near me?", and the assistants answer with named firms. They build that answer from what they can find: your website, your business listings, your reviews and mentions on other sites.

Electricians have an advantage here. Registration with a scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT usually comes with a public listing on a trusted site, which is just the kind of outside confirmation an assistant looks for. Questions electricians ask us

How long does SEO take for an electrician?

Plan for three to six months for results to build. Fixes to your Google Business Profile and website often show first, within weeks. Specific searches such as EICRs or EV chargers in your town tend to move sooner than the broad "electrician" searches, which take longer.

Is SEO worth it for a one-person electrical business?

It can be, because a sole trader only needs a steady handful of good enquiries to stay busy. The aim is not the most traffic. It is the right jobs in the right area, coming to you directly, so you rely less on paid leads.

Can I rank in towns where I am not based?

To a degree. Google Maps results lean on distance, so you rank best near your base. Your website can still rank in the normal results for other towns, if it has a useful page for each one. We set realistic targets for both.

Do reviews really affect where I rank?

Yes. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. They also decide who gets the call once you appear. We set up a simple request that goes to every customer after a job.

I am listed on my scheme's website. Is that enough?

It helps, but it is not enough alone. A scheme listing confirms you are registered. It does not tell Google which jobs you want or which towns you cover. Your own website and Google profile do that work.
