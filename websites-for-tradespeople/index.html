Do tradespeople need a website?

Yes, if you want work to come to you directly. A Facebook page or a directory profile helps, but you do not control either one, and on a directory your profile sits next to your competitors. Your own website is the one place online that is fully yours.

It also does a job that social pages cannot. Google uses your website to decide which searches to show you for. AI assistants like ChatGPT read it to work out what you do and where you work. Without a site, neither has much to go on.

A website earns its keep when:

  • You want enquiries that come to you alone, not shared with other firms.
  • You want more of your best-paying jobs, not just any job.
  • Customers look you up before they ring, and want proof you are real.
  • You want to rely less on paying for every lead.

What should a tradesperson's website include?

A trades website has one job: turn a visitor into a phone call or a quote request. Everything on it should help with that. These are the parts that matter.

What the site needsWhy it matters
A tap-to-call number on every pageMany visitors are on a phone and would rather ring than fill in a form.
A page for each serviceLets Google and AI match you to searches like "boiler repair" or "consumer unit upgrade".
A page for each area you coverGets you shown for searches with a town name in them.
Photos of your own jobsShows the work is real. Stock photos do the opposite.
Reviews and accreditationsGoogle reviews and badges such as Gas Safe, NICEIC or TrustMark answer the trust question quickly.
A short quote formCatches the people who look in the evening or cannot ring.
Fast loading on a phoneA slow page loses the visitor before they have seen anything.
Structured dataA label in the code that tells Google and AI exactly what your business is.

How does a website get a tradesperson recommended by ChatGPT and other AI assistants?

When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity for a roofer or an electrician nearby, the assistant gathers what it can find about local firms and names a few. Your website is the main source it has about you in your own words.

A site built for AI search does four things:

  • Says it plainly. What you do, where you work and how to reach you, written in text and not hidden in pictures.
  • Answers real questions. Short, direct answers to what customers ask, which an assistant can quote.
  • Carries structured data, so machines do not have to guess what your business is.
  • Matches everywhere else. The same name, area and phone number on Google, Bing, Apple Maps and the trade directories.

The site is half of it. Assistants also lean on what other sites say about you, such as reviews and listings, so we work on those alongside the build. This is called AI visibility, or generative engine optimisation, and it is part of every ASC plan.

How long does a trades website take to build?

About ten days from the first call with us. The usual hold-up on any build is waiting for photos and details, so it helps to have these ready:

  • Your logo, if you have one.
  • Photos of recent jobs, taken on a phone is fine.
  • A list of your services and the areas you cover.
  • Accreditation and membership details.
  • Your domain login, if you already own a domain.

Being found takes longer than being built. Plan for three to six months for search results to build. Fixes to a Google Business Profile often show first, and rankings for competitive searches follow.

I already have a website. Do I need a new one?

Not always. If your site is fast, works well on a phone and has a page for each service and area, we improve it. If it is slow, thin or built on a platform that blocks the basics, we rebuild it as part of the plan, and you keep your domain.

Do I still need a website if I am on Checkatrade or Facebook?

They are worth having, but they are rented space. The platform sets the rules, and on a directory your profile sits next to your competitors. Your own website is the one place that is fully yours, and it is what Google and AI assistants read to work out what you do and where.

Will my website show up in ChatGPT?

It can. AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity name local firms when people ask who to call.
