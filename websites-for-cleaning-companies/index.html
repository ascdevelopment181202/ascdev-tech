Websites for cleaning companies

Websites for cleaning companies have three customers to win. One wants a regular cleaner at home. One needs a one-off job done by a set date. One is a business looking for a contract. What does a cleaning firm's website have to do?

Cleaning is sold on trust and on routine. The customer is handing over a key, or the alarm code for an office. So the site has to answer a different first question for each kind of customer.

CustomerWhat they are buyingWhat the site should show first
A householdA weekly or fortnightly cleanWho comes, what gets cleaned, the days you have free and how to start.
A one-off jobEnd of tenancy, deep clean, oven, carpets or an after builders cleanWhat is included, how soon you can come and a quick way to get a quote.
A businessOffice, shop or communal area cleaning on a contractInsurance, how staff are checked and supervised, and how to book a site visit.

A single page headed "our services" serves none of the three well. We plan the layout around the customers you want more of.

What pages should a cleaning company's website have?

Each kind of clean you want more of gets its own page. That lets Google and AI assistants match you to a specific search, such as an oven clean.

  • Home. What you clean, where you work and one clear way to get a quote.
  • Regular house cleaning. What a visit covers room by room, whether you bring the products, and how the first clean works.
  • End of tenancy cleaning. The full list of what is cleaned, and what happens if the agent or landlord finds something missed.
  • Deep cleans and after builders cleans. How they differ from a regular visit.
  • Carpet, upholstery and oven cleaning, if you offer them, each with before and after photos of your own jobs.
  • Office and commercial cleaning. The kinds of premises you look after, the hours you can work and how a contract starts.
  • Area pages. One for each main town you want work in.
  • About and reviews. The people, the checks you carry out and what customers say.

Leave out any service you do not offer.

How does a cleaning website show that your staff can be trusted?

The customer may not be in the room while the work is done. Proof matters more here than a slogan, so say exactly what you do.

  • Criminal record checks. GOV.UK describes the basic DBS check as a criminal record check for people working in England and Wales. A person can request one for themselves, or an employer can ask for it. If your cleaners have one, say so, and say how often it is renewed.
  • Insurance. Name the cover you hold, such as public liability, and offer to send the certificate.
  • Keys and alarm codes. Say how keys are stored and labelled, and who is allowed to hold them.
  • The same cleaner each visit, if that is how you work, and what happens when they are away.
  • Reviews that name the job, such as a weekly clean kept up for a year or a flat handed back on time.

Only claim what is true for every cleaner you send. "All staff DBS checked" has to mean all staff.

What do business customers look for before they ask for a quote?

An office manager is choosing a supplier, so the commercial page should read like a short answer to a tender.

State who supervises the cleaners and how a missed clean is reported and put right. State the hours you can work, such as early mornings or evenings.

Say how you handle cleaning chemicals. The HSE says the COSHH regulations require employers to assess the risks that arise from the use of hazardous substances. Employers must also give staff information, instruction and training about those risks. A client may ask to see that this is done. A line saying your assessments and training records are available on request answers the question early.

If you belong to a trade body, show it. The British Institute of Cleaning Science describes itself as the largest independent, professional and educational body within the cleaning industry. Show a membership or a training certificate only if you hold it.

What should a cleaning quote form ask?

A good form lets you price the job, or book a visit, without three calls back and forth. It should be short enough to fill in on a phone.

  • Postcode, so you know at once whether you cover it
  • House, flat or business premises
  • Number of bedrooms and bathrooms, or the rough size of an office
  • Regular clean or one-off job, and which kind
  • How often, and which days suit
  • For an end of tenancy clean, the date the keys go back
  • Anything extra, such as an oven, carpets or inside windows

We set up the form with the site, along with missed-call text-back. If you are mid clean and cannot pick up, the caller gets a text straight away, so they know you will ring back.

Which searches should a cleaner's website be built around?

People search for the kind of clean plus the place. So the site is planned around your services and your towns.

  • "Cleaner near me" and "house cleaning" plus your town
  • "End of tenancy cleaning" plus your town
  • "Deep clean" and "after builders clean" searches
  • "Oven cleaning" and "carpet cleaning" plus your town
  • "Office cleaning" and "commercial cleaners" plus your town

Should a cleaning company put prices on its website?

Where you can, yes. An hourly rate for regular cleans, or a "from" price for an oven clean or an end of tenancy clean, helps a household decide to get in touch. For office contracts, say that you visit the site first and then send a written quote.

I clean on my own. Is a website worth having?

It can be. A small site still gives people who were given your name somewhere to check you out. Say plainly that it is you who turns up, which days you have free and which streets or towns you cover. If your diary is full, the site can say that too.

Should house cleaning and office cleaning share one website?

Yes, if one business does both. Give each its own section, with its own pages, proof and quote form. A facilities manager and a busy parent ask different questions, so neither should have to read the other's page to find an answer.

Can the website show the areas and days I cover?

Yes. We build a page for each main town you want work in, and the site states your working days and hours. If you only take regular clients in certain postcodes, the site says so. That saves you enquiries you would have to turn down.
