How long does local SEO take for a tradesperson?

Local SEO takes weeks for the first changes to show and months for results to build. Google says some changes take effect in a few hours and others could take several months. For a trade business starting with a thin Google profile and website, ASC Development works to a target of three to six months of steady work before judging the result.

By ASC Development · Updated 22 November 2026

Why is there no single answer?

Because local SEO is not one job. It is a string of separate jobs, and each one runs on its own clock. Verifying a profile is quick. Earning fifty honest reviews is not.

Google does not give a figure either. Its SEO starter guide says every change takes some time to show on Google's end. Some changes might take effect in a few hours, and others could take several months. It suggests waiting a few weeks before you judge whether a piece of work has helped.

So the honest answer comes in two parts. The first changes can show within weeks. The full result builds over months. How many months depends on where you start, how many firms you are up against, and how steadily the work is done.

What can change in the first few weeks?

The early jobs are the ones that depend on you more than on Google. They are also the ones a customer sees first.

  • The profile gets verified. Google says the review takes up to five working days.
  • The profile gets filled in. The right main category, each service by name, the towns you cover, opening hours and real photos.
  • The website goes live and Google starts to read it. New pages can be picked up within days, though it can take weeks.
  • The first new reviews arrive, if you start asking straight away.

None of this is a promise of more calls in week three. It is the groundwork. A firm that skips it has nothing for the later months to build on. Our guide to ranking on Google Maps goes through the profile steps one by one.

What takes months, and why?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is the part you can fix early, by telling Google clearly what you do and where. Distance you cannot change. Prominence is the slow one.

Google describes prominence as how well known a business is. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help. You cannot fill that in on a form. It has to be earned, one finished job at a time.

  • Reviews come at the speed you finish jobs and ask for them. Our guide to getting more Google reviews covers a simple routine.
  • Website pages for each service and each town take time to write well, and then time for Google to crawl and weigh up.
  • Mentions on other sites, such as a trade body or a supplier, come as you ask for them.

A firm that has traded in the same town for years may already have some of this in the bank. A new firm, or one that has never asked for a review, starts further back.

What slows local SEO down?

Some delays are outside your control. These ones are not.

  1. Putting off verification. It is the first step, and the wait for Google's review only starts when you do.
  2. Asking for reviews in bursts. Ten in one week and then none for three months leaves long gaps with nothing new.
  3. Changing everything at once. If the name, category, website and phone number all change in the same week, you cannot tell which change helped or hurt.
  4. Details that do not match. One phone number on the profile and another on the website gives Google two stories about the same business.
  5. Stopping and starting. Work done for a month and then dropped has little time to show.
  6. Trying to rank far from your base. Distance counts, so a town an hour away is a harder target than your own.

How can you tell it is working before the phone rings more?

Calls are the point, but they can be the last thing to move. There are earlier signs, and you can check each of them yourself.

  • Your pages are on Google. Search for your business name and a service. If the page for that service shows, Google has read it.
  • Reviews are arriving each month, not only when you remember to ask.
  • You show for the trade and the town, not only for your own name. Check on a phone, from inside the area you cover.
  • The call button and enquiry form on your website get used. Note where each enquiry came from.
  • New customers say they found you on Google. Ask every caller how they found you and keep a simple tally.

If none of these has moved after a few months of steady work, something needs changing. Do not just wait longer.

Questions about how long local SEO takes

Can anyone guarantee a top spot on Google by a set date?

No. Google's own advice on hiring an SEO says no one can guarantee a number one ranking on Google. A firm can promise the work it will do and when. It cannot promise where Google will place you or on what date.

Can I pay Google to speed up my local ranking?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Adverts are bought separately. The normal map results have to be earned through your profile, reviews and reputation.

Does asking Google to recrawl my site make it faster?

It tells Google the page is there, which is worth doing once. Google says crawling can take from a few days to a few weeks. It also says that asking again and again will not make it quicker, and that a request does not guarantee the page will be shown.

How long does it take to verify a Google Business Profile?

Google says verification reviews take up to five working days and that on rare occasions they may take longer. The wait only starts when you apply, so do this step first.

Should I stop the work once I rank well?

That is your choice, but the things that got you there keep moving. Other firms in your area carry on adding reviews, photos and pages. A profile and website that are kept up to date give Google and customers a reason to keep choosing you.
