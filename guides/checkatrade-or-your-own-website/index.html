Checkatrade or your own website: which is better for a tradesperson?

For most tradespeople the answer is both, in a set order. A directory such as Checkatrade can bring enquiries quickly while you are new or quiet. Your own website takes longer, but the enquiries it brings are yours alone and do not stop when a membership ends. A sensible plan uses a directory for work now, builds the website for work later, and shifts the balance as the website grows.

By ASC Development · Updated 12 November 2026

What is the real difference between a directory and your own website?

A directory or lead site is rented space. You pay to appear in front of customers the platform has already gathered. It can work fast, because the visitors are there from day one. But the platform sets the rules and the price, and your profile sits in a list beside your competitors.

Your own website is space you own. Nobody else is on it. It costs time or money to build and takes months to be found. Once it is found, the enquiries come to you directly and keep coming.

Neither one is a con and neither is a magic fix. They are different tools, and the useful question is which one suits where your business is now.

When does a directory make sense?

  • You are just starting out and need work this month, not in six.
  • You have a gap in the diary and want to fill it quickly.
  • You are moving into a new area where nobody knows you yet.
  • Your trade is one customers want vetted, and the directory's checks reassure them.

A good directory profile is also a listing in its own right. It is one more trusted site confirming your name, area and phone number, which helps Google and AI assistants trust the rest of what they find about you.

When is your own website the better choice?

  • You are tired of paying for every lead, or of chasing jobs that five other firms were also sent.
  • You want better jobs, not just more. A website lets you put your best-paying work first.
  • You get work by recommendation. People who are recommended to you look you up, and a website is what they expect to find.
  • You want to be found by name on Google, on Google Maps and in AI assistants.
  • You are building something to last, or to sell one day. A website and its rankings are an asset of the business. A directory membership is not.

Can you use both together?

Yes, and many established firms do. The two support each other when they are set up properly.

  1. Year one: lean on the directory for enquiries while the website is built and starts to rank.
  2. As the website grows: watch where each enquiry comes from. Ask every caller how they found you, and write it down.
  3. Later: keep paying for whatever still earns its fee, and cut back what does not.

Two practical tips. Use the same business name, area and phone number on the directory and on your website. And when a directory customer is happy, ask them for a Google review as well, so the goodwill builds up on a profile you own.

How do you work out which is paying its way?

Keep a simple tally for three months. For each source of work, note three numbers:

  • What it cost you.
  • How many enquiries it brought.
  • How many of those became paid jobs, and what they were worth.

The cost for each job won is the figure that matters, not the cost for each lead. A cheap lead that five firms fight over can cost more per job than a dearer one that comes only to you. Questions about directories and websites

Is Checkatrade worth it for a new tradesperson?

It can be. A new business has no reviews and no rankings, so a known directory can bring early work and lend some trust. Check the current fees and terms, track what each job costs you to win, and start building your own website alongside it.

Do I still need a website if I am on Checkatrade?

Yes. Customers often look a firm up after seeing it on a directory, and a website is what they expect to find. It is also the only way your own name, not the directory's, shows in Google results and AI answers.

Why do I get so many poor leads from lead sites?

On some platforms the same job is offered to several firms, so you compete on speed and price. Customers there may also be comparing several quotes. Your own website tends to bring fewer enquiries of better quality, because the customer chose to contact you.

Will my Checkatrade reviews show on Google?

Not as Google reviews. They stay on the platform where they were left. That is why it is worth asking happy customers for a Google review too, so your own profile builds up.

How long before a website replaces paid leads?

Plan for three to six months before a new website brings steady enquiries, and longer in a competitive area. Most firms cut paid leads back step by step as their own enquiries grow, not all at once.
