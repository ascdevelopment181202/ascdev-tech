The state of UK electrician websites 2026

UK electrician websites get the basics right and often skip the extras. ASC Development checked 70 homepages on 22 November 2026. 97% loaded over https and 83% had a tap to call link, but only 26% had LocalBusiness schema and 21% showed a review score or count as text. The median homepage weighed 2.31 MB.

By ASC Development · Updated 22 November 2026

How do electricians compare with plumbers?

This is our second trade study. The first looked at 68 plumber homepages eight days earlier. Same directory, same ten cities, same checks.

Read the table with care. Each sample is about 70 firms, so a gap of a few points could be chance. The measuring script was rebuilt for this run, which can shift a borderline match either way.

What was recorded on each homepage?

One visit per site, in Chromium, at a phone screen size of 390 by 844 pixels. We opened the address on the listing, let the page settle, and noted six things. Nothing beyond the homepage was opened and nothing was retried.

  • Secure address. Does the browser end up on https?
  • Phone layout. Is there a viewport tag setting width or scale?
  • Calling. Is there a phone link that dials, or at least a number in the text?
  • Schema. Is there structured data of the LocalBusiness type or one of its narrower types, such as Electrician?
  • Reviews. Does the text mention reviews, and does it give a score or a count?
  • Weight. How much data arrived before the page went quiet, up to eight seconds after loading?

Security and phone layout are close to universal here. The drop comes lower down the chart. Eight homepages (11%) had all four items in the last row and a review figure as well.

Can a customer call an electrician straight from the page?

On 58 of the 70 homepages, yes: the number was a link that dials. Seven more printed the number as plain text, so a visitor on a phone has to copy it or type it. On five we found no number in the page text at all.

26 listings still gave an old http address. 24 of those sites moved the visitor to https by themselves, and two stayed on http. One homepage had no viewport tag.

A phone link is one short piece of code, and it is the item where electricians led plumbers by the widest margin in our two samples.

Do electricians' sites say what the business is in code?

Mostly not. 18 of the 70 homepages (26%) carried LocalBusiness schema. Seven of those used the Electrician type, ten used the general LocalBusiness type and one used a building trade type.

45 homepages had some structured data in the JSON-LD format. So 27 sites went to the trouble of adding structured data and still did not describe a local business with it. Their code described a website, a web page or, on 16 of them, an organisation in general terms.

Schema states plain facts in a fixed format: name, phone, area, hours, services. Our SEO for electricians page sets out where it sits beside a Google Business Profile and reviews.

How many electrician sites put a review figure on the page?

15 of 70 (21%). These gave a score, such as a rating out of five, or a count, such as a number of five star reviews. Five homepages had rating schema.

Many more talked about reviews without a figure. 51 homepages (73%) mentioned reviews or testimonials somewhere: a menu link, quoted comments, a request to leave a review, or a widget from a review service.

A widget can show a score that our text check cannot read, so the share of visitors who see a figure may be higher than 21%. The guide on getting more Google reviews covers building the record itself.

How much data does an electrician's homepage use?

The middle homepage used 2.31 MB across 63 requests. The smallest used 0.10 MB and the largest 31.0 MB.

What can this study not tell you?

  • It is not a random sample. All 70 firms chose to list on one directory, in ten cities. Northern Ireland is absent.
  • One visit, one evening. A site that failed at that moment was left out, not marked down. Four of the 15 unreadable sites returned a loading error that may have been on our side.
  • Homepage only. Reviews, schema or a phone link may sit on other pages.
  • Text only. A number or a rating shown inside a picture or a widget was not counted.
  • Mixed firms. Some do solar, heating or building work too. A firm stayed in if its homepage mentioned electrical work.
  • The plumber comparison is rough. Different day, rebuilt script, small samples.

What should an electrician do with these numbers?

If your site is on https and fits a phone screen, you match nearly every firm here. The room to stand out is further down:

  1. Add LocalBusiness schema of the Electrician type. 52 of 70 homepages had none.
  2. Write your review score and count on the homepage, and name the platform. 55 of 70 did not.
  3. Turn the phone number into a link. 12 of 70 had no link that dials.
  4. Weigh the homepage. Eight were over 5 MB. Photos are the first place to look.

Questions about the electrician website study

How many electrician websites were checked?

70. We began with 113 listings on the electrician pages for ten UK cities on Which? Trusted Traders. After removing repeats, firms with no website and sites we could not read, 70 homepages that mentioned electrical work were left.

Are any electrical firms named?

No. Only totals are published. The list of firms stays private and no single firm's result appears anywhere, because the point is the overall picture.

What do electrician websites miss most often?

A review score or count shown as text: 55 of the 70 homepages (79%) had none. LocalBusiness schema was missing on 52 of 70 (74%). A tap to call link was missing on 12 of 70 (17%).

Are electrician websites better than plumber websites?

Not across the board. In our two samples, electricians were ahead on tap to call links, 83% against 72%. Plumbers were ahead on LocalBusiness schema, 37% against 26%, and their homepages were lighter. Both samples are small, so treat gaps of a few points as level.

How do I check my own electrical website?

Open the homepage on your phone. Look for https at the start of the address, press the phone number to see if it dials, and look for your review score written on the page. Then ask whoever built the site whether it carries schema of the Electrician type.
