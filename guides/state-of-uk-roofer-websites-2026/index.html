The state of UK roofer websites 2026

The state of UK roofer websites 2026, in short: the basics are in place and the extras are patchy. ASC Development checked 53 homepages on 28 November 2026. 92% loaded over https and 77% had a tap to call link. 45% had LocalBusiness schema and 34% put a review figure in the page text. The middle homepage weighed 1.62 MB.

By ASC Development · Updated 28 November 2026

From the public roofer listings on Which? Trusted Traders. We read the first page of results for ten cities on 28 November 2026. They were Sheffield, Liverpool, Bristol, Cardiff, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Leeds, Manchester, Birmingham and London.

What was recorded on each homepage?

Each roofing site got a single visit from a Chromium browser set to a phone sized screen, 390 pixels wide and 844 tall. The browser went to the web address shown on the directory profile and stayed on that first page. A site that failed was not tried a second time.

  • Https. The address the browser finished on, after any redirect.
  • Viewport tag. Whether the page tells a phone how wide to draw it.
  • Phone number. Whether it appears in the text, and whether pressing it starts a call.
  • Business schema. Any LocalBusiness structured data, including narrower types such as RoofingContractor.
  • Review figure. Any mention of reviews, and any score or count written out.
  • Page weight. Data received by the end of an eight second wait after load.

Can a customer ring a roofer straight from the page?

Usually. 41 of the 53 roofing homepages had a number you can press to dial. Ten showed a number that does nothing when pressed, so a customer on a phone has to copy the digits by hand. Two had no number anywhere in their text.

The directory profile for 27 firms began with http. 23 of them redirect to the secure address. The other four served the page on plain http. Two homepages lacked a viewport tag.

Four sites left on http is a small count, but it is the one row where roofers sat below both other trades.

Do roofers' sites say what the business is in code?

Nearly half do. 24 of the 53 homepages (45%) carried LocalBusiness schema. Eleven of those used the RoofingContractor type, eight used the general LocalBusiness type and five used the wider home and construction type.

JSON-LD of some kind was on 44 homepages. That leaves 20 with structured data that never says "this is a local roofing firm". Ten of those 20 marked up an Organization. The others described things such as the site and its pages.

For a roofer the useful facts to mark up are the trading name, the phone number, the towns covered and the kinds of roof work done. SEO for roofers explains how that fits with the rest of the work.

How many roofing sites put a review figure on the page?

18 of 53 (34%). Each wrote out a rating, a number of reviews or both. Several said where the figure came from, such as Google, Checkatrade or Which?.

Eight homepages had rating schema. Three of those eight showed no figure in the page text, so the rating sat in the code and not in front of the reader.

Reviews came up in some form on 37 homepages (70%), often as customer quotes with no total. Our check reads text, so a score drawn inside a third party widget can be missed. If you need more reviews before you can show a figure, start with how to get more Google reviews.

How much data does a roofer's homepage use?

The middle homepage used 1.62 MB across 82 requests. The smallest used 0.21 MB and the largest 16.7 MB.

What can this study not tell you?

  • Who was picked. Roofers listed on a single directory, in ten large cities. None are in Northern Ireland. Firms outside it may look different.
  • Size. 53 homepages. Every percentage here carries a wide margin.
  • Sites we lost. 14 would not open in our browser. Two of those errors could have come from our own connection. Those firms are missing, not failed.
  • Other pages. A contact page with a dialling link, or a reviews page with a score, earns nothing here.
  • Pictures and widgets. A phone number in a banner image, or stars in an embedded panel, cannot be read as text.
  • What counts as a roofer. Any homepage whose title or text named roofing work. Some of these firms also fit windows and fascias or take on building jobs.
  • Three trades, three days. The plumber and electrician figures were gathered on other dates with earlier versions of the script.

What should a roofer do with these numbers?

A roofing site that works on a phone and shows a number is only level with the field. Five jobs, in the order the sample suggests:

  1. Put your rating and number of reviews in plain text, with the name of the review site. 35 of 53 had no figure.
  2. Add LocalBusiness schema of the RoofingContractor type. 29 of 53 homepages had none.
  3. Make the phone number dial when pressed. On 12 of 53 it did not.
  4. Check the address starts with https. Four sites were still on http.
  5. Slim down a heavy homepage. Eight passed 5 MB. Large job photos are worth checking first.

Questions about the roofer website study

How many roofer websites were checked?

53. The starting point was 107 roofer listings for ten cities on Which? Trusted Traders. Repeats, firms without a website, sites that would not open and homepages that never mentioned roofing were taken out.

Are any roofing firms named?

No. The page gives counts and percentages only. We keep the list of roofers and their individual results to ourselves.

What do roofer websites miss most often?

A review figure. 35 of the 53 homepages (66%) wrote no rating or review total in their text. Next came LocalBusiness schema, absent from 29 (55%), then a phone number that dials, absent from 12 (23%).

How do roofers compare with plumbers and electricians?

In our three samples, roofers were ahead on LocalBusiness schema, 45% against 37% for plumbers and 26% for electricians. They were also ahead on showing a review figure. They were behind on https, at 92%. With samples this small, a difference of a few points means little.

How do I check my own roofing website?

Load it on a phone. Check the address starts with https. Press your number and see whether the phone offers to call. Look for your rating in words and digits, not only stars. Last, ask your web designer if the homepage has RoofingContractor schema.
