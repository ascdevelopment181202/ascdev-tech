Google Business Profile checklist for trades

This Google Business Profile checklist for trades has 20 checks in five groups: set-up, where you work, what you do, proof and upkeep. Each check is tied to what Google's own help pages say. A plumber, electrician, roofer or builder can work through it in one sitting. ASC Development manages Google Business Profiles for trades as part of its monthly plan.

By ASC Development · Updated 24 November 2026

What does the checklist cover?

Google says that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local search results. That line comes from its tips to improve your local ranking. The checklist turns it into 20 things you can tick off.

You need to be signed in to the Google account that manages the profile. Google says it reviews changes before they go live, so an edit may not show at once.

The list does not promise a ranking. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and how well known the business is. A full and accurate profile is the part you control most directly. Our guide on how to rank on Google Maps explains all three.

Checks 1 to 4: is the profile set up properly?

These come first because the rest depends on them. The rules are in Google's guidelines for representing your business.

No.CheckWhat to do
1The profile is verifiedGoogle says photos and videos only show after you verify, and that verifying lets you manage your business information.
2There is only one profileSearch for your business name and your old names. Google's guidelines say there should only be one profile per business.
3The name is your real nameUse the name on your van and invoices. Google does not permit unnecessary information in the name, so no towns or services added on.
4The phone number and website are yoursGoogle says the number must be under the direct control of the business. Link to your own website, not to a page that sends people elsewhere.

Checks 5 to 8: does it show where you work?

A tradesperson who travels to customers is what Google calls a service-area business. Its page on managing service areas sets out how that works.

No.CheckWhat to do
5The address is right for your kind of businessIf customers do not come to you, Google says to remove the address from the profile and enter only a service area. If customers do visit your premises, you can show the address and a service area as well.
6Service areas are named placesSet them by town, city or postcode. Google says you cannot set a radius around your business.
7There are no more than 20, and they are accurateGoogle allows up to 20 service areas and asks you to be as specific and accurate as possible.
8The whole area is realisticGoogle's tip is that the overall area should not be more than about two hours of driving time from your base.

Listing a town does not mean you will rank there. Distance still counts.

Checks 9 to 12: does it say what you do?

This group is what lets Google match the profile to a search such as "boiler repair" or "fuse board upgrade".

No.CheckWhat to do
9The main category is specificPick the closest match to your core trade. Google says to choose categories that are as specific as possible but representative of your main business.
10Extra categories are fewGoogle says to use as few categories as possible. Add another only if it is work you really do.
11Every service is listedGoogle's page on managing your services says you can pick from suggested services or add your own, with a description. Keep prices and phone numbers out of service names.
12The description is writtenSay what you do, where and for whom, in plain words. Google's limit is 750 characters, with no web addresses.

Checks 13 to 16: does it prove the work?

Photos and reviews are what a customer can check before they ring. This group makes sure there is something real to see.

No.CheckWhat to do
13A logo and a cover photo are setGoogle's photo guidance says the logo helps customers recognise you and the cover photo should best represent the business.
14Photos are of your own jobsUse JPG or PNG, in focus and well lit. Google says a photo should represent reality, with no heavy filters.
15You ask customers for reviewsMake it part of finishing the job. Our guide on getting more Google reviews covers how, and Google's rules on asking.
16Every review has a replyGoogle says replying to reviews shows that you value the feedback. Keep replies short and polite, good review or bad.

Checks 17 to 20: is it kept up to date?

Google warns that if your business information is not accurate, the profile might not show for relevant searches in your area.

No.CheckWhat to do
17Opening hours are trueShow the hours you answer the phone and take work. Only show 24 hours if someone really picks up at night.
18Holiday hours are setGoogle's special hours are for holidays and other short changes. Confirming them, even when they match your normal hours, makes it clear to customers that they are accurate.
19The website matches the profileSame business name, same phone number, same services and towns. A page on your site for each service backs up the list in check 11.
20Someone looks at it every monthAdd new photos, reply to reviews and check that nothing has changed. Put a reminder in the diary.

Questions about Google Business Profiles

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google says you can manage how your business shows up on Maps and Search at no charge. Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Should a tradesperson show a home address on Google?

Not if customers never come to you. Google's guidelines say a service-area business should hide its business address from customers. You list the towns or postcodes you cover instead, and the profile shows those.

How many service areas can I add?

Up to 20. Google says to set them by city, postcode or another type of area, and that you cannot set a radius around your base. It also advises that the whole area should not stretch more than about two hours of driving time from where you are based.

How many categories should a trade business choose?

As few as describe the business. Google's guidelines say to use as few categories as possible for your overall core business, and to choose ones that are as specific as possible. For one trade that can mean a single main category plus one or two that truly apply.

How often should I go through this checklist?

Go through all 20 checks once, then look again whenever something changes: a new phone number, a new service, a new town or different hours. Photos, review replies and holiday hours need little and often. A quick look each month is a sensible habit.
