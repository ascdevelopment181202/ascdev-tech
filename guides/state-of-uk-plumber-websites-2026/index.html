The state of UK plumber websites 2026

ASC Development checked the homepages of 68 UK plumbing firms on 14 November 2026. 96% loaded over https and 97% had a mobile viewport tag. Only 37% had LocalBusiness schema, 72% had a tap to call link and 29% showed a review score or count as text. The median homepage weighed 1.68 MB.

By ASC Development · Updated 22 November 2026

Which plumber websites did we check?

We used the public plumber listings on Which? Trusted Traders, a public directory of traders run by Which?. On 14 November 2026 we took the first page of plumbers for ten cities: London, Birmingham, Manchester, Leeds, Glasgow, Edinburgh, Cardiff, Bristol, Liverpool and Sheffield. That gave 120 listings.

How did we check each website?

Each homepage was opened once in the Chromium browser, set to a phone size screen of 390 by 844 pixels. We followed the address given on the listing, waited for the page to load, and recorded what was there. No site was visited twice and no page other than the homepage was opened.

  • Https: the address in the browser after loading starts with https.
  • Mobile viewport tag: the page has a viewport tag that sets the width or scale for phones.
  • LocalBusiness schema: the page carries structured data of the type LocalBusiness or a more specific type such as Plumber or HVACBusiness.
  • Tap to call: the page has at least one phone link that dials when pressed.
  • Reviews: the visible text mentions reviews or testimonials, or shows a review score or count.
  • Page weight: the data transferred to load the homepage, counted until the page went quiet.

The basics are nearly universal in this group. The gaps are elsewhere: structured data, a phone link that dials, and a review score shown in plain text. Ten of the 68 homepages (15%) had all four of the items in the last row and also showed a review score or count.

How many plumber websites are secure and set up for phones?

65 of the 68 homepages finished on an https address. 41 of the listings still pointed at an old http address, and on 38 of those the site passed the visitor on to https by itself. Three stayed on http.

66 homepages had a mobile viewport tag and two had none. The MDN guide to the viewport tag explains that without it, some phones lay a page out in a virtual window wider than the screen. Google says in its mobile-first indexing guide that it uses the mobile version of a site's content for indexing and ranking. So the phone version of a page matters for search as well as for visitors.

The weaker spot is calling. 49 homepages had a tap to call link. 13 more showed the number as plain text only. Six had neither, and on at least two of those the number was there only as part of a picture.

How many use LocalBusiness schema?

25 of the 68 homepages (37%) had LocalBusiness schema. 13 of those used the Plumber type, 3 used HVACBusiness, 7 used the general LocalBusiness type and 2 used a building trade type. 50 homepages had structured data in the JSON-LD format, so half of those had it without describing a local business at all.

Schema is a short block of code that states facts about a business in a fixed format. Google's local business structured data guide says it lets you tell Google about details such as business hours. It sits alongside a Google Business Profile and does not replace one.

For a plumber, the useful facts are the business name, phone number, address or area covered, opening hours and the services offered. Our SEO for plumbers page covers where schema fits with the other local search work.

How many plumber websites show their reviews?

47 of the 68 homepages (69%) mentioned reviews or testimonials in some way. That includes a menu link to a reviews page, a line asking customers to leave a review, quoted comments, and review widgets from outside services.

Far fewer gave a figure. 20 homepages (29%) showed a review score or a review count as text on the page, such as a rating out of five or the number of reviews on a named platform. Five had rating schema.

On the other 48 homepages no score or count appeared in the page text. Some of those show one inside a widget from a review platform, which a text check does not read, so the true figure for what a visitor sees is somewhat higher. Our guide on getting more Google reviews as a tradesperson covers how to build the record in the first place.

How heavy are plumber homepages?

The median homepage transferred 1.68 MB over 60 requests. The lightest was 0.14 MB and the heaviest was 19.8 MB.

More data takes longer to arrive on the same connection, so weight is a fair first sign of how a page will feel on a phone with a weak signal. It is not the same as speed, which we did not measure. Seven homepages were still loading when our wait ran out, so their true weight is higher than the figure we recorded.

What are the limits of this study?

  • It is a small sample from one directory. Every firm chose to be listed there, so this is not a random cross section of UK plumbers. Northern Ireland is not covered.
  • It is one visit on one day. A site that was down or slow at that moment was recorded as we found it.
  • 14 sites could not be read. Some sites turn automated visitors away. Those sites may be perfectly good for a normal visitor, so we left them out and did not count them as faults.
  • Only the homepage was checked. A firm may show reviews or schema on other pages.
  • Text only. A phone number or review shown inside a picture was not counted.
  • Some firms do more than plumbing. A firm was included if the directory listed it under plumbers and its homepage mentioned plumbing.

What should a plumber take from this?

If your site already loads over https and works on a phone, you are level with nearly everyone in this sample. The places to pull ahead are the ones where fewer than three in four sites were in order:

  1. Make the phone number a link that dials. 19 of the 68 homepages had none.
  2. Add LocalBusiness schema, using the Plumber type, with your name, phone, area, hours and services. 43 of 68 had none.
  3. Put your review score and count on the homepage as text, with the platform named. 48 of 68 did not.
  4. Check the weight of your homepage. Start with the photos, and check that each one is no larger than it needs to be.

Questions about the plumber website study

How many plumber websites did the study check?

68. We started from 120 listings on the plumber pages for ten UK cities on Which? Trusted Traders. 98 listed their own website. Of those, 68 homepages loaded for us and mentioned plumbing, and those 68 are the sample.

Does the study name any plumbing firm?

No. The figures are totals only. We do not publish the list of firms or any result for a single firm, because the aim is to show the overall picture, not to mark individual businesses.

What was the most common gap on plumber websites?

No review score or count shown as text on the homepage: 48 of the 68 sites (71%). Next came no LocalBusiness schema, on 43 of 68 (63%), and no tap to call link, on 19 of 68 (28%).

Is a 1.68 MB homepage too heavy?

There is no single pass mark, and we did not measure loading speed. 1.68 MB was the middle value in this sample. Nine homepages came in under 1 MB, and four were over 5 MB, so there is a wide spread between the lightest and the heaviest.

Can I check my own website the same way?

Yes. Open your homepage on a phone. Check the address starts with https, the page fits the screen without pinching, the phone number dials when you press it and your review score is on the page as text. Schema is hidden in the code, so ask whoever built the site whether LocalBusiness schema is in place.
