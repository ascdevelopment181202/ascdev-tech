Pay monthly websites for tradespeople, explained

A pay monthly website spreads the cost of a trades website over a regular fee, with little or nothing to pay up front. The fee usually covers the build, hosting and updates. It can be a good deal, as long as you know who owns the domain, what happens if you leave, how long you are tied in and whether anything is being done to get the site found.

By ASC Development · Updated 12 November 2026

What is a pay monthly website?

It is a website you pay for month by month, in place of one large bill at the start. A company builds the site, hosts it and looks after it, and you pay a set fee for as long as you use it.

The appeal for a tradesperson is clear. There is no big sum to find before the site has brought in a single job, and someone else deals with the technical side. The cost is known and regular, like van insurance.

The catch is that "pay monthly" describes how you pay, not what you get. Two offers at a similar price can be very different underneath. One may be a template with your name on it. Another may include real work each month to bring in enquiries. The rest of this guide is about telling them apart.

What should a pay monthly website include?

ItemWhat to look for
The buildPages written for your services and areas, not a template with the blanks filled in.
Hosting and securityIncluded in the fee, with the site kept fast and backed up.
Your domainRegistered in your name, or clearly yours to take away.
ChangesA fair number of updates included, such as new photos, prices or services.
Mobile layoutBuilt for phones first, with a tap-to-call button.
Getting foundSome plans include SEO and Google Business Profile work. Many do not.
SupportA named person you can reach, not only a ticket form.

The "getting found" row is the one that changes the value most. A website nobody visits is a cost. A website that brings in a job or two a month pays for itself.

What are the catches to watch for?

  • Long tie-ins. Some plans run for a fixed term of a year or more. Check the length and what it costs to leave early.
  • Who owns the domain. If the provider registered it in their own name, moving away can be awkward. Your domain should be yours.
  • What happens to the site if you stop. With many monthly plans the site comes down when the payments end. That is normal, but you should know it in advance and be able to take your own words and photos with you.
  • Extra charges for small changes. Ask what is included before you need it.
  • A site and nothing else. If the plan includes no work on search, expect to do that yourself or pay for it separately.
  • Setup fees in the small print. "No upfront cost" should mean exactly that.

None of these makes a plan bad. They are things to know before you sign, not after.

What questions should you ask before signing up?

  1. Is there a minimum term, and what does it cost to leave?
  2. Whose name is the domain registered in?
  3. If I leave, can I take my domain, my text and my photos?
  4. How many changes are included each month?
  5. Is any SEO or Google Business Profile work included, and what exactly?
  6. Will I get a report showing calls, enquiries or visitors?
  7. Who do I speak to when I need something?
  8. Can I see two or three sites you have built for my trade?

A good provider will answer all eight without hedging. If the answers are vague, that tells you something too.

Is pay monthly better than paying once?

It depends on what you would do with the site afterwards.

  • Paying once suits you if you have the money to spare now, want to own every part of the site outright, and have someone to keep it updated and work on its search rankings.
  • Paying monthly suits you if you would rather keep the cash in the business, want the upkeep handled, and want the people who built the site to stay responsible for whether it works.

Over several years a monthly plan can cost more in total than a one-off build. What you are buying with the difference is the ongoing work. Questions about pay monthly websites

Are pay monthly websites worth it for tradespeople?

They can be, if the plan includes work to get the site found and the terms are fair. A monthly site that brings in even one extra job a month can cover its cost. A monthly site with no visitors does not, however cheap it is.

Do I own a pay monthly website?

It varies. With many plans you are paying to use the site, and it comes down if you stop. What matters most is that the domain name is yours and that you can take your own text and photos with you. Ask before you sign.

Why are some pay monthly websites so cheap?

Usually because they are built from a template with very little written for your business, and include no work on search. That can be fine as an online business card. It is unlikely to bring in enquiries by itself.

Can I cancel a pay monthly website?

That depends on the provider's terms. Some have a minimum term and some run month to month. Check the minimum term, the notice period and any leaving fee before you sign.
