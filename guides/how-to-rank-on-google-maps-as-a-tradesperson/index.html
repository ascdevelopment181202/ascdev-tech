How to rank on Google Maps as a tradesperson

To rank higher on Google Maps, a tradesperson needs a complete and accurate Google Business Profile, the right category, a full list of services and areas, a steady flow of reviews, and a website that matches the profile. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance, so the work goes into the other two.

By ASC Development · Updated 12 November 2026

How does Google decide who shows on the map?

When someone searches "plumber near me", Google shows a map with a few businesses listed under it. Many people pick from those few, so being one of them is worth a great deal.

Google explains how it chooses in its own guide to local ranking. It names three things:

FactorWhat Google means by itCan you change it?
RelevanceHow well your profile matches what the person searched for.Yes, with categories, services and a full profile.
DistanceHow far your business is from the person searching.No.
ProminenceHow well known your business is, including reviews and links from other websites.Yes, over time.

Google also states plainly that you cannot pay for a better local ranking. The map results are earned.

What are the eight steps to ranking higher?

  1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile. It is free. Until it is verified you cannot fully manage it.
  2. Choose the right main category. Pick the one that is closest to your core trade, such as "Plumber" or "Electrician", then add others that genuinely apply.
  3. Fill in everything. Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show. Opening hours, phone, website, description, the lot.
  4. List each service by name. Boiler repair, consumer unit upgrade, flat roofing. These help Google match you to specific searches.
  5. Set your service areas. If you travel to customers, list the towns you cover.
  6. Add real photos regularly. Finished jobs, the van, the team. A phone photo is fine.
  7. Ask every customer for a review, and reply to each one. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.
  8. Make your website back it up. A page for each service and area, with the same name, area and phone number as the profile.

How should a tradesperson who works from home set up the profile?

Most tradespeople do not have a shop. Customers do not visit you, you visit them. Google allows for this with what it calls a service-area business.

  • You give Google your address so it can verify you, then choose not to show it publicly.
  • You list the areas you serve instead, by town or postcode.
  • Your profile then shows those areas, not your home.

This is the right setup for a tradesperson working from home, and it keeps your address private. Bear in mind that distance still counts, so you will tend to rank best in the area around your base. Use your real business name and a real address. A made-up one puts the whole profile at risk.

How do you get more Google reviews without breaking the rules?

Make asking part of finishing the job. Google suggests sharing a link or QR code that takes customers straight to your review page, and you can find both in your profile.

  • Ask on the day, while the customer is pleased with the work.
  • Send the link by text, so it takes one tap.
  • Ask everyone, not only the customers you think will be kind.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and briefly.
  • Never offer a discount or gift for a review. Google's rules prohibit incentives. Its advice is in its tips to get more reviews.

A steady trickle of reviews, week after week, looks better to customers and to Google than a burst of twenty in one weekend followed by silence.

What part does your website play in Maps ranking?

Your profile and your website work as a pair. Google says prominence is based partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. A proper website gives Google much more to go on than a profile alone.

  • Service pages confirm the services listed on your profile.
  • Area pages confirm the towns you cover.
  • Matching details confirm that the profile and the site are the same business.
  • Mentions and links from other sites, such as your trade body, suppliers or local groups, add to how well known you are.

If your site is thin or missing, that is usually the next thing to fix after the profile itself. Our checklist of what a trades website should include covers it.

How long does it take to rank on Google Maps?

It depends on where you start and who you are up against. Filling in a half-empty profile can make a difference within weeks. Building reviews and a strong website takes months. In a large town with established firms, plan for three to six months of steady work before you judge the result.

There is no shortcut, and anyone who promises the top spot by a set date is guessing. What works is doing the eight steps properly and keeping them up.

Questions about Google Maps ranking

Can I pay Google to rank higher on Maps?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can buy adverts, which are labelled as ads, but the normal map results are earned through your profile, reviews and reputation.

Why does a competitor with fewer reviews rank above me?

Reviews are only one part of it. They may be closer to the person searching, have a better matched category, list their services more fully or have a stronger website. Check your profile against theirs point by point.

Can I rank in a town I am not based in?

It is harder, because distance counts. You will usually rank best near your base. You can still add the town as a service area and build a page for it on your website, which helps in the normal results under the map.

Should I put keywords in my business name on Google?

No. Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your van and paperwork. Describing your services belongs in the categories, services and description sections of the profile.

How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Little and often. Add photos as you finish jobs, reply to reviews as they arrive, and check your hours and services whenever something changes. An active profile shows customers the business is alive.
