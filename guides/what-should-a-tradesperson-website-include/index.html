What should a tradesperson's website include?

A tradesperson's website should include a tap-to-call phone number on every page, a page for each service and each area, photos of your own work, reviews, your accreditations, a short quote form, and structured data that tells Google and AI assistants what your business is. It needs to load fast on a phone. Everything else is optional. The full checklist of twelve is below.

By ASC Development · Updated 12 November 2026

What is a trades website actually for?

One thing: turning a visitor into a phone call or a quote request. It is not a brochure and it is not there to win design awards. Every part of the page should either help someone decide to contact you, or help Google and AI assistants understand what you do and where.

That test makes most decisions easy. If something on the site does neither job, it can go.

What are the twelve things every trades website needs?

1. A tap-to-call number on every pageMany visitors are on a phone and want to ring, not hunt for a contact page.
2. What you do and where, in the first screenA visitor should know within seconds that you do their job in their area.

What should go on a service page?

A service page is where a search turns into an enquiry, so it is worth getting right. Each one should cover:

  • What the job is, in the words a customer would use.
  • What is included, and what is not.
  • How it works: the visit, the quote, the job and the tidy-up.
  • Roughly how long it takes.
  • Photos of that job, done by you.
  • A review or two from customers who had that job done.
  • The areas you cover for it.
  • A call button and a quote form at the bottom.

Aim for a page a customer could read in two minutes and come away knowing what happens next.

What should you leave off a trades website?

  • Stock photos of smiling models in clean overalls. Customers can tell.
  • Sliding banners. They slow the page and hide your message.
  • A long "welcome to our website" paragraph. Say what you do instead.
  • One services page listing everything. It gives Google nothing specific to rank.
  • Badges for schemes you have left. Careful customers check.
  • Music, pop-ups and video that plays by itself.
  • A contact form with ten boxes. Name, phone, postcode and the job is enough.

What does a website need for Google and AI search?

The same things that help a customer help a search engine: clear pages, plain words and real detail. Google says its AI answers have no extra requirements beyond a page being indexed and following normal good practice, and that no special files are needed. That is in its guide to AI features and your website.

In practice that means:

  • Text that is written out on the page, not locked inside images.
  • Headings that say what each section is about.
  • Structured data that matches what the page shows.
  • Nothing that blocks search engines from reading the site.

The rest of the work happens off the site, in your Google profile, your reviews and your listings. Our guide on ranking on Google Maps covers that side.

How can you test your own website in five minutes?

  1. Open it on your phone, on mobile data. Count how long before you can read it.
  2. Hand it to someone who does not know your business. After five seconds, ask what you do and where.
  3. Try to ring yourself. Is the number on screen, and does tapping it start a call?
  4. Search Google for your main job plus your town. Is there a page on your site about exactly that?
  5. Send yourself a quote request. Does it arrive, and how quickly?

Questions about trades websites

How many pages should a tradesperson's website have?

As many as you have services and main areas, plus home, about, reviews and contact. A one-page site can look tidy, but it gives Google very little to match to specific searches.

Do I need a blog on my trades website?

Not a blog for its own sake. What helps is a small set of pages that answer real customer questions, such as how long a job takes or what affects the cost. A few good answers are worth more than weekly posts nobody reads.

Should a tradesperson's website show prices?

Where you can, yes. A call-out charge, a day rate or a "from" price helps customers decide to get in touch and filters out poor fits. If every job is quoted, explain how quoting works and how fast you reply.

Is a Facebook page enough instead of a website?

A Facebook page is worth having, but it is not a replacement. You do not control it, and Google and AI assistants rely far more on a proper website to work out what you do and where you work.

Can I build a trades website myself?

Yes. Website builders make it possible if you have the time to write the pages and keep them up to date. The site is only half the job, though. Getting it found takes ongoing work on your Google profile, reviews and listings as well.
