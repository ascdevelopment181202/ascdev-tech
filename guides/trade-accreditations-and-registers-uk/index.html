Trade accreditations and registers UK

Trade accreditations and registers UK customers can check come in three kinds. Some are tied to the law or to Building Regulations, such as Gas Safe Register. Some are trade bodies that vet their members, such as the Federation of Master Builders. Some are public records, such as Companies House. ASC Development lists 23 here, each with a link to its public search.

By ASC Development · Updated 25 November 2026

What kinds of register are there?

There are three kinds. The first is tied to the law or to Building Regulations, as with gas work. The second is a trade body that sets its own entry rules. The third is a public record, such as the list of limited companies.

Every row below links to the body's public search. The third column is taken from that body's own website, which we read on 25 November 2026. Where a fact comes from a second page, the row links to that page as well.

A listing shows that a firm met the body's bar. It is not a promise about any one job.

Which registers cover gas, heating and renewables?

Gas is the one trade here where the register is the law. Gas Safe Register says that by law all gas businesses must be on it.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
Gas Safe RegisterGas workIt calls itself "the official list of businesses that are legally permitted to carry out gas work". Check by business number or trading name, or by an engineer's licence number. It warns that not all engineers are qualified for all types of gas work.
OFTECOil, solid fuel and renewable heatingA register of technicians, searched by postcode, company name or registration number. OFTEC says technicians take an approved course and assessment and are inspected regularly.
HETASSolid fuel and biomass appliancesHETAS says its installers are trained and assessed. It also says a solid fuel installation in England and Wales must by law be notified to building control.
MCSSmall renewables such as solar panels and heat pumpsSearch by installer name or certification number to check that a firm is MCS certified. Results can be filtered by Boiler Upgrade Scheme registration.

Which registers cover electricians?

NICEIC and NAPIT both appear on the GOV.UK list of competent person scheme operators, which is explained further down. SELECT is the trade body for Scotland.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
NICEICElectrical workNICEIC says registered firms are regularly assessed by its assessors. Its guide to checking says to search the business name, or a sole trader's own name.
NAPITElectrical, heating, plumbing and ventilationNAPIT says its member search verifies the status of a scheme member's certification, and that members are regularly assessed.
SELECTElectrical contractors in ScotlandSearch by postcode, category of work or company. SELECT says every applicant has work inspected by one of its technical advisers.

GOV.UK also points to an Electrical Competent Person Register. Its site would not load for us, so it has no row.

Which registers cover plumbers?

These four overlap: WaterSafe says its members come through schemes that include APHC, the CIPHE and SNIPEF.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
WaterSafePlumbers and water supply specialistsSearch by postcode. WaterSafe says members have specific training in the water fittings regulations and carry public liability insurance. It covers England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
CIPHEPlumbers and heating engineersSearch by postcode, street or town. The CIPHE describes the people listed as verified by the professional body for the industry.
APHCPlumbing and heating contractors in England and WalesSearch by town and by type of work. APHC says all members are vetted before they are approved.
SNIPEFPlumbing and heating firms in Scotland and Northern IrelandIts Need a Plumber site lists SNIPEF approved firms. Its membership criteria ask for a year of trading, insurance and a qualified lead operative.

Which registers cover builders, roofers, glaziers and scaffolders?

Each of these bodies sets its own entry rules.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
Federation of Master BuildersSmall and medium-sized building firmsSearch by trade, job type or builder name. The FMB says members have to pass a vetting and inspection process before joining.
NFRCRoofing contractorsSearch by postcode or company name. The NFRC says members pass a vetting procedure with an office and site audit, then are inspected every three years.
FENSAReplacement windows and doors in homes, England and WalesSearch by postcode, installer name or registration number. FENSA says its approved installers can self-certify that their work complies with Building Regulations.
NASCScaffolding and accessSearch by organisation name, postcode or region. NASC calls itself the UK's leading trade body for scaffolding and access. The pages we read do not set out how members are checked.

Which registers cover locksmiths, pest control, trees, gardens, decorating and kitchens?

None of these six said, on the pages we read, that the law requires membership.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
Master Locksmiths AssociationLocksmithsSearch by postcode. The MLA describes its approved master locksmiths as vetted, inspected and qualified.
British Pest Control AssociationPest controlThe BPCA says the companies in its search have all been audited to BS EN 16636, which it calls the British standard in pest management.
Arboricultural AssociationTree surgeonsSearch by business name or postcode. It says every Approved Contractor has been assessed in tree work and in health and safety.
BALILandscapers and garden designersFilter by domestic or commercial work. BALI says a vetting officer visits firms that apply to be accredited contractors.
Painting and Decorating AssociationPainters and decoratorsSearch by category of work from its home page. The PDA says members are vetted for quality workmanship and follow a code of practice.
BIFISFitted kitchens, bedrooms and bathroomsThe British Institute of Fitted Interiors Specialists. Search by postcode or validate a registration number.

Which registers work for any trade?

Two checks apply whatever the trade.

RegisterCoversWhat its own site says
TrustMarkWork in and around the homeTrustMark calls itself the only UK Government-Endorsed Quality Scheme. It licenses and audits scheme providers, who license their own members.
Companies HouseAny limited companyGOV.UK says some details are free to look up, including the registered address, the date of incorporation, current and resigned officers and insolvency information.

What does it mean when an installer can self-certify?

Some building work has to meet Building Regulations. GOV.UK says an installer registered with a competent person scheme can self-certify that their work complies with building standards. That is instead of getting building regulations approval.

The GOV.UK list of current schemes names the operators. They include NICEIC, NAPIT, OFTEC, HETAS, FENSA, APHC and the National Federation of Roofing Contractors, through a scheme called CompetentRoofer.

The same page says Gas Safe Register is not technically a competent person scheme. In practice, it says, Gas Safe installers have the same responsibilities for complying with building regulations.

GOV.UK points to separate guidance for Scotland and Northern Ireland, so do not assume these schemes work the same way there.

How should a tradesperson show these on a website?

Show only what you hold today. Then make each one easy to check:

  • Put the logo near the top of the page and beside the quote form.
  • Give your registration or membership number in plain text next to it.
  • Link the logo to the body's public search, so the customer can look you up.
  • Say what it covers in one line, for example which kinds of gas work you are registered for.
  • Take a logo down the day a membership lapses.

Our list of what a tradesperson's website should include covers the rest of the page. The pages on websites for heating engineers and SEO for builders go further into the registers for those two trades.

What are the limits of this list?

Keep these in mind:

  • It is not complete. There are other bodies, and two sites we tried would not load, so they have no row.
  • Each row repeats what the body says about itself. We have not tested any of those claims.
  • Bodies change their rules and their web addresses. Check the body's own site before you rely on a row.
  • A firm that is not in a trade body is not a bad firm for that reason. The Arboricultural Association says tree surgeons outside its scheme may be equally competent.
  • ASC Development is not connected to any body on this page.

Questions about trade registers

Does a tradesperson have to be on a register by law?

It depends on the trade. Gas Safe Register says that by law all gas businesses must be on it. The trade bodies on this page set their own entry checks, and none of them said on the pages we read that the law requires membership.

How do I check that a gas engineer is registered?

Use the check on the Gas Safe Register website. You can search by the business registration number or trading name, or by the licence number on the engineer's ID card.

What is a competent person scheme?

GOV.UK says an installer registered with a competent person scheme can self-certify that certain types of building work comply with building standards. That is instead of getting building regulations approval. The scheme operators it lists include NICEIC, NAPIT, OFTEC, HETAS, FENSA and APHC.

Do these registers cover Scotland and Northern Ireland?

Some do and some do not. SELECT covers Scotland. SNIPEF covers Scotland and Northern Ireland. APHC and FENSA describe their work in terms of England and Wales. WaterSafe names all four nations. Check the body's own site for where you live.

Should I put accreditation logos on my website?

Only the ones you hold today. Next to each logo, give your membership or registration number and link to the body's public search, so a customer can check it in one tap. A customer cannot rely on a logo they cannot check.
